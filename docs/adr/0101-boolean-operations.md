# ADR 0101: Boolean operations on shapes

## Status
Accepted (v0.17.0).

## Decision
**Union, Intersect, Subtract and Exclude overlap** combine the selected shapes (two or more paths that are not locked) into one compound path, as one undo step. The result takes the look of the shape at the back (fill, outline, width, join, cap, opacity, blend) and its place in the stacking order, replaces the operands and is selected. *Subtract* takes every shape in front away from the one at the back; the other three are folded pair by pair. An open sub-path is closed by a straight line, as when it is filled; each operand's own fill rule (non-zero or even-odd) decides what is inside it. The operands are put in the coordinates of the back shape exactly (the anchors differ by an exact amount, the rest is one rounding), so shapes made at different zoom levels, in different frames, turned, combine. Nothing left, shapes too different in size (a ratio past double range) and outlines that graze in a way the tolerances cannot tell apart are said in a notice and change nothing.

**Curves stay curves** (`src/doc/boolean.ts`, pure functions). The result is made of pieces of the original Bézier segments, not of a flattened copy, so it is as smooth at any zoom as the operands were (the new nodes are where the outlines cross).

1. *Crossings.* Each segment of one operand against each of the other (bounding boxes first). Two lines: the exact formula, with the ends of a shared stretch when they run along each other. Otherwise the curves are halved until the pieces that can still touch are within a coarse tolerance, the candidates that follow each other are one meeting, and each is made exact by Newton's method on the pair of parameters from three starts (so two crossings close together are both found); a meeting where the curves only touch, where Newton has nothing to hold on to, is the closest approach of the two, found by looking from one curve to the other.
2. *Cutting.* Each segment is cut at its crossings exactly (de Casteljau). A straight segment is given evenly spaced control points while it is worked on (a cubic with handles on its ends does not run evenly along its length, so a cut at t would not be where t says) and is written back as a line.
3. *Choosing.* Every piece is asked one question: is the result on one side of you and not on the other? The answer is the winding number of each operand at a point a hair to the left and to the right of the middle of the piece, counted on the curves (crossings of a ray with each monotone piece), put through the operation. A piece inside an operand, outside it, along the edge of the other one, or an interior edge of a shape that overlaps itself all come out of the same rule. A piece with the result on one side only is kept and turned so the result is on its left; a piece of the second operand that lies on the outline of the first is the same edge the first already offers and is skipped.
4. *Joining.* Pieces are joined end to end into closed loops (matched by position, in a grid), so the loops run the right way round, holes the other way. A node is where a piece starts; its handles are those of the pieces it joins, none for a line.

**Scale.** Everything is decided per group of loops that cross each other, with tolerances from the size of that group (a hair is 10^-9 of it, a sliver 10^-6), not from the size of the whole selection, so a shape 10^-10 times the size of the one it sits in is a hole and not a speck lost in the tolerance of the big one (tested 30 levels apart in the browser). Every tolerance has a floor of a few dozen steps of the numbers themselves (a shape 220 units from the origin cannot be told a hair to the left of, in doubles, at 10^-17).

**Limits.** The outlines of the result are exact where they were the operands', but a crossing is placed to about 10^-14 of the size of the shapes (the curves cross at irrational places), which is invisible until zoomed in a hundred trillion times. Features of an interacting group smaller than 10^-6 of its size are dropped (slivers of a graze). Curves that lie along each other over a stretch (two copies of one curve, one cut off the other) are settled by their ends and the sides; curves lying along each other only over part of their length can leave a loop that will not close, which is reported. An operand that crosses itself is handled by the sampling, but a loop of it that cannot be seen from either side is lost.

**In the app.** A "Combine shapes" button on the selection bar when two or more shapes are selected, opening the shared menu with four items, each with its own icon (squares-unite, -intersect, -subtract, -exclude; the button is `combine`).

## Tests
`tests/unit/boolean.test.ts`: random pairs (200 operations, 12,000+ points) and triples of rectangles, ellipses, polygons, stars and quads, in random places and turns, against the set operation computed from the operands' own outlines at random points away from every outline; and the cases that break such code: two overlapping squares (corner counts of each result), a circle and a rectangle (arcs stay arcs, straight sides have no handles), a hole, nesting, identical shapes, shapes that do not meet, shapes sharing an edge, circles touching at a point, a rectangle along a tangent, even-odd operands, open paths. `tests/e2e/vectors.spec.ts`: all four through the real menu on overlapping shapes with pixel checks, one undo step, curves kept, the notice when nothing is left, and shapes 30 levels apart in size. Chromium and Firefox.
